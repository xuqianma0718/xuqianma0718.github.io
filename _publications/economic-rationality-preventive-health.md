abstract: >-
  Using the Understanding America Study, I compare two measures: quantitative ability, combining numerical and financial-literacy tests, and choice consistency, measured by whether incentivized choices under risk can be rationalized by stable preferences. I link both measures to influenza vaccination reports from 2014–2026 and COVID-19 vaccination during the 2021 rollout. Results differ by setting. For influenza, reasoning measures have little average association with vaccination but become more positive after 2020, with stronger evidence for choice consistency. During the COVID-19 rollout, quantitative ability becomes more positively associated with receipt, whereas choice consistency becomes less positive during eligibility expansion. These findings motivate further research on the respective roles of understanding risk information and coherently weighing competing benefits and costs in vaccination decisions, and on how standard health surveys can measure these skills.
