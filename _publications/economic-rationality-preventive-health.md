abstract: >-
  I test whether two measures of quantitative reasoning are more strongly associated with influenza vaccination after the pandemic began than before it, and how their associations with COVID-19 vaccination change during the 2021 rollout. Using the Understanding America Study, I examine quantitative ability, assessed through numeracy, number-series, arithmetic, and financial-literacy tests, and choice consistency, measured by whether incentivized choices under risk can be rationalized by a stable preference ordering. I link them to 2014–2026 influenza vaccination reports and COVID-19 vaccination during the rollout. For influenza, choice consistency becomes more positively associated with uptake after 2020, while quantitative ability shows a similar but statistically insignificant change. During the rollout, quantitative ability is positively associated with uptake in later phases, whereas choice-consistency associations become less positive during eligibility expansion. These findings motivate research on understanding risk information, coherently weighing competing benefits and costs, and how standard health surveys can measure these skills.
