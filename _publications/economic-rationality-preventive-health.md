abstract: >-
  Health economists often proxy decision-making ability with cognitive tests. Using the Understanding America Study, I compare test-based quantitative ability with incentivized revealed-preference choice consistency as predictors of flu (2014–2026) and COVID-19 (2021) vaccination. Neither predicts flu vaccination before 2020, but in both political parties consistency predicts its decline, mostly after the pandemic: by 2025–2026, vaccination had fallen 9 percentage points among the least consistent third while rising among the most consistent. In the COVID-19 rollout, the measures part ways: quantitative ability predicts earlier vaccination once eligibility expands; consistency predicts neither. The measures thus point to different policies. For new vaccines, lowering the cost of acting, as with pre-booked appointments, can narrow skill gaps. For flu, universal interventions that make the routine choice easier are preferable, since education, cognitive test scores, and political party poorly identify those drifting away.
