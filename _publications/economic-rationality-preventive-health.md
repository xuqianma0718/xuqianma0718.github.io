## Abstract

Measuring quantitative reasoning may help explain differences in vaccine uptake across individuals and over time. I test whether different measures are more strongly associated with vaccine uptake after the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic than before it. Using the Understanding America Study, I examine two candidate measures: quantitative ability, assessed through numerical and financial literacy tests, and choice consistency, measured by how closely incentivized financial choices align with stable preferences. I link these measures to reported influenza vaccination from 2014–2026 and COVID-19 vaccination during the 2021 rollout. Influenza results provide suggestive support: choice consistency becomes more positively associated with uptake after 2020, while quantitative ability shows a similar but statistically insignificant change. During the COVID-19 rollout, quantitative ability is positively associated with uptake in later phases, whereas choice consistency shows no consistent positive association. These findings motivate further research on how standard health surveys can best measure the quantitative reasoning skills relevant to health decisions.
