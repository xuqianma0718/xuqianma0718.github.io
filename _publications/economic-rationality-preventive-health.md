abstract: >-
  Health economists often proxy decision-making ability with cognitive tests. Using the Understanding America Study, I compare test-based quantitative ability with incentivized revealed-preference choice consistency as predictors of flu (2014–2026) and COVID-19 (2021) vaccination. Neither measure predicts flu vaccination before 2020. Vaccination then diverges by consistency, mostly after the pandemic and within both political parties: by 2025–2026, it had fallen 9 percentage points among the least consistent third but held steady among the most consistent. In the COVID-19 rollout, the measures part ways: quantitative ability predicts earlier vaccination once eligibility expands and accounts for a third of the widening college gap; consistency predicts neither. The measures point to different policies. For new vaccines, lowering the cost of acting, as with pre-booked appointments, can narrow skill gaps. For flu, universal interventions that ease the routine choice are preferable, since targeting by education, cognitive test scores, or political party would miss many who drift away.
