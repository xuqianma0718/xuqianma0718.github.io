## Abstract

Which dimension of decision-making predicts vaccination, and when? Using the Understanding America Study, I link two incentivized revealed-preference consistency indices—Afriat’s CCEI (e<sup>*</sup>) and dominance-augmented e<sup>**</sup>—and quantitative reasoning to self-reported influenza vaccination from 2014–2026 and self-reported COVID-19 vaccine receipt during the 2021 rollout. The key finding is comparative: decision-making measures are not interchangeable. For influenza, pooled choice-consistency associations are small, but one standard deviation higher e<sup>**</sup> is associated with a 2.1-percentage-point larger post- versus pre-2020 vaccination difference (p = 0.037), 3.4 percent of the pre-2020 mean. Conditional on quantitative reasoning, the e<sup>**</sup> change is 1.9 points (p = 0.073), while the quantitative-reasoning change is similar in magnitude but less precise (p = 0.138). During the COVID-19 rollout, quantitative reasoning has clear positive associations in later phases, whereas choice-consistency estimates are mixed. Preventive health behavior therefore depends on which decision-making margin is relevant in a given vaccination environment.
