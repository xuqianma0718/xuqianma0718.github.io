abstract: >-
  Quantitative reasoning may be associated with differences in vaccine uptake, and these associations may change over time. I test whether two measures of quantitative reasoning are more strongly associated with influenza vaccination after the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic than before it, and how their associations with COVID-19 vaccination change during the 2021 rollout. Using the Understanding America Study, I examine two candidate measures: quantitative ability, assessed through numeracy, number-series, arithmetic, and financial-literacy tests, and choice consistency, measured by how closely incentivized choices under risk can be rationalized by a stable preference ordering. I link these measures to reported influenza vaccination from 2014–2026 and COVID-19 vaccination during the 2021 rollout. For influenza, choice consistency becomes more positively associated with uptake after 2020, while quantitative ability shows a similar but statistically insignificant change. During the COVID-19 rollout, quantitative ability is positively associated with uptake in later phases, whereas choice consistency shows no consistent positive association. These findings motivate further research on the respective roles of understanding risk information and coherently weighing competing benefits and costs in vaccination decisions, and on how standard health surveys can measure these skills.
